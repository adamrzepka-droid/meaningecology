Mission

To study the history of the social conditions for the production of new meaning, in order to assess, preserve and foster those conditions in the present.

The challenge

Humanity’s relationship to the making of meaning is changing, as generative artificial intelligence takes over a growing share of what people write, read and think with.

The danger in that change is the rapid levelling of the landscape of human symbolic life, and with it the loss of the social and symbolic ground in which revolutionary insight is cultivated.

An ecological comparison helps. Picture everything that humanity constantly says and writes as the living ecology of a mountain rainforest. Generative AI is at present working on that forest like a strip-mining and ranching operation combined: flattening its terrain, extracting and processing its resources, collapsing its life cycles and drastically reducing its diversity in the service of efficient production. The variety of life is not the only casualty. The absorption of shocks, adaptation, even evolution come to a halt.

The evidence

The comparison may sound alarmist. It is both predictable and increasingly documented.

A 2025 study by researchers at the University of Southern California linked the spread of language models as writing assistants to marked declines in linguistic diversity. A 2026 study by researchers at Carnegie Mellon, Oxford, MIT and UCLA found that AI assistance improves immediate performance while reducing persistence and the ability to work unaided. As these systems consume a growing proportion of their own products, the effects of recursion and flattening are already visible.

What is at stake is the capacity to think in new ways about every other challenge we face: the application of creative insight, rooted in the long history of human meaning, to the discovery of new solutions.

The work

The Institute applies advanced computational tools, detached from a profit motive and detached from marketing, to understand how innovative thinking has emerged from particular social and intellectual formations in the past, and to develop ways of protecting, sustaining and cultivating such formations now.

This is a new iteration of the goals and methods that have always defined humanist inquiry. It has two projects under way.

Archival Intelligence

Reads the past. An instrument for recovering lost networks of intellectual exchange from the manuscript record.

Daedala

Watches the present. A research vessel that gathers current research and draws the futures that follow from it.
