Daedala

A research vessel for the ecology of meaning

Daedala is a speculative instrument. It gathers what the research currently shows about how Generative AI affects the variety and richness of human expression — semiodiversity — and maps possible futures accordingly. As new studies arrive, its settings change. The name is Lucretius’s word for the inventive variety of nature.

The living network of human meaning, 2026 to 2100

Filaments are the channels through which people make and share meaning. Their colors reflect variety; their fine branching shows local difference. Adjust the sliders across the range of effects that current research posits, and see possible effects on the global ecology of human meaning for the rest of the century.

2026

Futures

Automation

Small · 30%Large · 85%
Basis

The automated share of what is produced. No study fixes where this ends up; both ends are assumptions. Each region’s starting point is real: the automated share of its web traffic, from Cloudflare Radar. Adoption by country follows Liu & Wang 2026. One estimate put AI-generated pages at about 14 percent of Google results by September 2024 (cited in Burden et al. 2024). Use is still shallow: in Morikawa 2024 (RIETI DP 24-E-084), 8.3 percent of Japanese workers used AI at work, up from 5.8 percent a year earlier, for about 15 percent of their tasks.

Stylistic narrowing

By a sixthBy half
Basis

Sourati et al. 2025: when AI rewrote people’s texts, variety of style fell by 16 to 51 percent. Moon et al. (preprint): in 372,793 admissions essays, variety of words rose while variety of ideas fell. Kosmyna et al. 2025 (preprint): essays written with AI converged on the same names, phrases and topics.

Capacity erosion

Slow · 2% a yearFast · 10% a year
Basis

Rismanchian et al. 2026: students’ time on AI-solvable problems fell 2.8 percent a quarter, and retention fell with it. Strömberg, Lei & Wu 2026 (CEPR DP21577): exam scores down a fifth within six months. Liu et al. 2026: worse performance alone after ten minutes of help. Kosmyna et al. 2025 (preprint, 54 people): 15 of 18 who wrote with AI could not quote their own essay minutes later, and the effects carried over when they wrote unaided. Against this, Lira et al. 2025 (preprint): people who practiced writing with AI wrote better alone a day later, apparently by imitating its examples. Skill can rise while variety narrows, so the slow end stays an assumption.

Recursion

ContainedRunaway
Basis

Burden et al. 2024 describe how systems trained on their own output degrade (“model collapse”), and how hard that output is to filter out once it is in circulation. Here the effect is slight until machine-made material approaches half of what circulates, then steepens, and it does not reverse: a tipping point, like carbon released from thawing permafrost. Where the threshold lies is an assumption.

Confidence

CalibratedInflated
Basis

Shaw & Nave 2026 (working paper): people followed a deliberately wrong AI four times in five, and their confidence rose and did not fall as errors mounted. That calibrated confidence acts as a brake is an assumption.

Concentration

Many systemsA few
Basis

Burden et al. 2024 argue that human-written text from before 2022 is becoming a scarce input for training, which entrenches the few systems that already hold it. Centola 2022 finds that decentralized networks reliably improve collective judgment, while centralized ones help or harm depending on who sits at the center. Where the slider sits is an assumption. With a few systems, everything routes through the same hubs and settles on one color. With many systems and inflated confidence, each seals itself off: the strands between them die and the branches loop back on themselves.

Refugia

NoneThree in ten
Basis

The share working under protected conditions, which keeps its color. In Strömberg et al. about a fifth of users kept working as before and lost little; in Shaw & Nave people overrode a wrong AI one time in five; in Rismanchian et al. the effect vanished under proctoring.

How to read it

Color is variety: each filament line begins with its own hue, spread in proportion to a region's linguistic diversity. Brightness is activity: web traffic per person online, on a compressed scale. All colors are set to the same perceived brightness, so a brighter thread is a busier one, whatever its color. Fine filaments are local difference; as meaning flattens they thin out and what remains turns one color. Nothing is drawn at the meeting points: the bright places are where many threads cross. Heavier threads run between the busiest places. Waves of brightness travel along the threads, more of them and faster where web traffic per person is higher. The web fills in as more of each region comes online. About a third of the threads fork off another thread part-way along, as capillaries do. About a fifth go nowhere in particular and feather off at the edges; these carry the rarer colors, and they are the first to go as variety is lost. A few threads in every region reach in from its neighbors.

What moves it

Each year a region changes by its automation (the automated share of production) times its share of people online times the assumed narrowing. As independent capacity erodes, reliance grows and the loss deepens. Once machine-made material is close to half of what circulates, systems feeding on their own output can tip the loss into a steeper decline that does not reverse. Confidence decides whether that loop can be broken: when calibrated, noticed loss slows the erosion and lets capacity recover; when inflated, the loss goes unseen, and the network glows brighter as it bleaches. The most linguistically diverse regions are reached later but fall faster. Regions with more automated traffic today meet the automation curve sooner. Concentration speeds the change and decides where the long strands run.

Status

An illustration of an argument, not a measurement. The network is grown at random over a coarse map, and three starting values are real: share of people online (World Bank, from ITU figures), and traffic per person and automated share of traffic (Cloudflare Radar). Linguistic diversity by region is also real (Greenberg’s index by country, weighted by population). The settings are tethered to studies but interpreted freely: these are possible scenarios, not projections.
